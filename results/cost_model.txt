------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WRITE AMPLIFICATION - the number worth arguing about
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DynamoDB is 60% of the total, and profile writes alone are
  $938/month, because the model assumes one write per event.

  That assumption is a design choice, not a fact. If the stream job keeps
  per-visitor state and flushes on session end rather than per event, the
  write count drops by roughly the average events-per-session. The cost
  model is what surfaces that: it is not visible from the architecture
  diagram, only from the arithmetic.

SERVING-TIER TRIPWIRE - when Postgres stops being the right answer
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The MVP serves dashboards from pre-aggregated rollups in PostgreSQL,
  which this team already runs. That is a reversible decision. Here is the
  condition that reverses it, computed rather than asserted.

  1-minute buckets retained hot                  2,880   (48h)
  cartesian rows/bucket/tenant                    200   (paths x event types)
  events/bucket/tenant                             69   (traffic bound)
  rows/bucket/tenant = min of those                69
  BINDING CONSTRAINT                     event volume

  rollup rows per tenant                      200,000
  hot rollup rows, 500 tenants            100,000,000
  Postgres comfort ceiling                250,000,000   [Assumed]
  utilisation                                   40.0%
  headroom                                       2.5x

  Rollups are SPARSE. A tenant averaging 69 events a minute cannot fill
  200 (path x type) buckets, so row count is bounded by TRAFFIC, not by
  how many pages the customer tracks. Modelling the cross-product instead
  overstates this by ~3x and would have picked the wrong architecture.

  THE TRIPWIRE, three conditions. Any one fires a migration review:

    1. CAPACITY   hot rollup rows exceed 250,000,000, which at
                  current shape means ~2.5x growth in events per tenant.
                  Path cardinality only starts to matter above ~17 paths
                  per tenant (modelled at 50); below that, traffic binds
    2. LATENCY    dashboard query p95 exceeds 1.0s over a 7-day window
    3. FRESHNESS  rollup write lag exceeds 30s at p95

  Condition 1 is a leading indicator - it fires before customers feel
  anything. Conditions 2 and 3 are trailing: by then they already have.
  Alerting on 1 is the point of computing it.

  WHY DEFER RATHER THAN COMMIT: growth in events per tenant is what
  decides this, and the brief gives a fleet-wide total rather than a
  per-tenant distribution. 500 tenants averaging 100k events/day is a
  very different system from 20 tenants at 2M and 480 at 8k, and the
  brief does not say which it is. Committing to a columnar store on day
  one is not more rigorous than deferring - it is guessing with more
  confidence. The MVP ships on infrastructure the team already operates,
  and the threshold that would prove that wrong is instrumented in week
  one. Per-tenant distribution is the first thing I would measure.

  This is an option held, not a migration scheduled. It may never fire.
